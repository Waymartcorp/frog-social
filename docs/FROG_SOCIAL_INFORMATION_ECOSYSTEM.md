# Frog Social Information Ecosystem

## 1. Purpose

Frog Social is a structured troubleshooting, case-memory, and guided husbandry intelligence system for frog and Xenopus users.

It is not:
- a generic AI forum
- a flat chat app
- a pathogen-panic engine
- a freeform answer bot
- a simple archive of disconnected reports

It is:
- a persistent social observation layer
- an emerging-thread detection layer
- a direct case intake system
- a permanent case-history system
- a husbandry-guided AI interpretation system
- a stored knowledge environment that becomes more useful over time

Frog Social is meant to turn scattered observations, discussion, and experience into durable, structured, recallable troubleshooting intelligence.


---

## 2. Core Intelligence Thesis

Frog Social is a living troubleshooting and diagnostic environment.

It becomes more valuable over time because it accumulates and reuses:

- human observations
- persistent discussions
- formalized cases
- husbandry guidance
- pattern recurrence
- practical outcomes

The system does not learn by replacing human judgment.

It learns by preserving and organizing human experience through a husbandry-guided AI lens.

This means Frog Social is not just a chat system and not just a case archive.
It is a human + AI + husbandry-memory system.


---

## 3. The Frog Social Intelligence Pattern

Frog Social builds a new diagnostic and troubleshooting lineage by combining:

### PEOPLE
- direct observations
- replies
- suggestions
- follow-up comments
- disagreement
- prior experience
- practical knowledge that would otherwise disappear

### AI
- interpretation
- clustering
- summarization
- comparison
- thread detection
- recommendation shaping
- memory recall across posts and cases

### HUSBANDRY GUIDE
- MD files / master guidance
- stable husbandry doctrine
- priorities about what matters most
- principles that shape weighting and interpretation

### PAST CASES
- stored cases
- stored summaries
- case outcomes
- repeated patterns
- case-based memory of what happened before

These four together form Frog Social’s core intelligence pattern:

**People + AI + Husbandry Guide + Past Cases**

That combination is what makes the system valuable.

Frog Social has the potential to change Xenopus and frog husbandry worldwide by:

- preserving distributed practical knowledge that would otherwise be lost
- making good husbandry reasoning easier to access and reuse
- reducing repetitive confusion around recurring husbandry problems
- helping users recognize patterns earlier
- empowering smaller labs and less experienced users
- giving frog users a stronger troubleshooting and memory environment
- creating a lasting practical knowledge archive around frog care and colony issues

This is not just software.

It is an effort to create a durable, evolving, global husbandry memory and troubleshooting system for frog users.

## 5. How Frog Social Learns

Frog Social learns by accumulating and reusing four knowledge layers:

### 1. Live Human Discussion
- social posts
- replies
- clarifications
- follow-up observations
- competing explanations
- new practical suggestions

### 2. Stored Posts and Emerging Threads
- persistent social records
- topic clusters formed from recent discussion
- memory of how problems first appeared and evolved

### 3. Stored Cases in Case History
- formalized problem records
- case titles
- summaries
- dates and case numbers
- what was learned
- eventual outcomes and what worked

### 4. MD Files / Husbandry Master Guidance
- stable doctrine
- trusted system-level guidance
- weighting rules
- interpretation priorities
- what the AI should emphasize or de-emphasize

The LLM does not invent knowledge by itself.

Its job is to:
- interpret
- organize
- compare
- synthesize
- and help structure recommendations

It should do that using the four layers above.

Over time, Frog Social becomes a stored knowledge environment because:
- new chats are preserved
- new cases are stored
- new follow-up information can attach to cases
- updated husbandry guidance can influence future recommendations

## 6. Knowledge Hierarchy

Not all information in Frog Social should be treated equally.

### Highest authority
**MD files / husbandry master guidance**
- foundational system guidance
- carefully maintained doctrine
- stable husbandry truths and priorities

### Strong authority
**Strong prior cases**
- repeated patterns
- well-formed cases
- resolved cases
- cases that align with system guidance

### Medium authority
**Current case synthesis**
- current emerging thread interpretation
- current discussion synthesis
- case-in-progress understanding

### Lower authority
**Live chat suggestions / hypotheses**
- speculative ideas
- weakly supported guesses
- one-off suggestions
- LLM-assisted discussion content not yet reinforced

The system must not treat all content as equally trustworthy.

## 7. Core Objects

## 7.1 Social Post
A Social Post is a persistent message in the live social feed.

A post may contain:
- direct observation
- question
- suggestion
- prior experience
- LLM-assisted interpretation
- case recall
- measurement
- context

Social Posts:
- persist
- remain visible
- form the raw record of what was said

This raw layer is important and should not be discarded.

---

## 7.2 Social Session
A Social Session is the current live discussion container on the Social page.

A Social Session contains:
- persistent posts
- recent emerging threads
- up to 2 current active cases
- optionally temporary related/recalled cases

The Social Session is not the permanent archive.

It is the live workspace.

---

## 7.3 Emerging Thread
An Emerging Thread is a temporary topic cluster derived from recent posts.

Examples:
- feeding response
- biofilter / ammonia
- skin findings
- water source / chemistry
- stocking density
- flow / vibration

Emerging Threads:
- are temporary
- are based on recent posts
- are live working objects
- may move into background memory after aging out
- are the main unit from which Social-generated cases are created

A single social session may contain multiple emerging threads.

---

## 7.4 Current Case
A Current Case is a case created from a mature emerging thread in the current session.

Current Cases:
- appear on the Social page
- are limited to at most 2 visible cases
- remain visible for about 5 days
- later move off the Social page but remain in Case History

---

## 7.5 Archived Case / Case History Record
An Archived Case is a permanent formal case stored in Case History.

Each case must have at minimum:
- case number
- date
- title
- structured summary

Cases in Case History are durable memory objects.

---

## 7.6 Related / Recalled Case
A Related / Recalled Case is an archived case surfaced temporarily because:
- it overlaps with the current emerging thread
- or a user explicitly recalls it

These should be temporary in the Social UI:
- limited in number
- aged out after about 2–3 days
- returned to archive-only state afterward

If simplifying the system is necessary, similar-case auto-calling may be disabled temporarily.

---

## 7.7 MD Files / Husbandry Master Files
MD files are the stable doctrine layer.

They represent:
- foundational husbandry priorities
- system-level guidance
- stable interpretive rules
- product philosophy
- trusted guardrails

These are not casual notes.

They should be:
- carefully maintained
- periodically updated
- intentionally edited
- treated as system guidance, not disposable text

MD files are what steer the AI toward a husbandry-first lens.

---

## 7.8 Recommendation
A Recommendation is structured guidance produced by the system.

Recommendations may be shaped by:
- current discussion
- current case
- prior cases
- MD guidance
- LLM synthesis

Recommendations must not be presented as equally supported.
They need support distinctions.


---

## 8. Two Case-Creation Paths

Frog Social has two formal case-entry paths.

### Path A — Social path
1. Users post in Social
2. The system detects Emerging Threads
3. A mature Emerging Thread becomes a Case
4. The Case is stored in Case History

### Path B — Describe a Problem path
1. A user directly submits a structured problem
2. The submission may create a Case immediately
3. The Case is stored in Case History

Both paths end in the same permanent archive:
**Case History**

Describe a Problem is not separate from the case system.
It is a direct case-intake route into the same memory structure.


---

## 9. Describe a Problem as Direct Knowledge Input

Describe a Problem is one of the main ways Frog Social can be taught quickly and cleanly.

It allows a user to:
- intentionally describe a situation
- create a formal case directly
- inject structured knowledge into Case History without waiting for open social emergence

This is important because some valuable knowledge:
- may already be well formed
- may need to be entered directly
- may be easier to store as a clean case than as a chain of social posts

Describe a Problem is therefore one of the main truth-input paths for the system.


---

## 10. Persistence Rules

### Social Posts
- persistent
- remain visible in the Social feed
- are the durable raw interaction layer

### Emerging Threads
- temporary
- based on recent posts
- should not remain forever on the Social page
- may move into background memory after about 7 days

### Current Cases on Social
- max 2 visible
- remain on Social page for about 5 days
- then move off the live page and remain in Case History

### Related / Recalled Cases on Social
- temporary only
- may be surfaced when relevant
- age out after about 2–3 days
- do not remain forever on the page

### Case History
- permanent
- holds all formal cases
- durable archive of date + case number + structured summary


---

## 11. Follow-Up Window

Once a case is created, relevant new comments may still attach to it for about 7–10 days.

This allows:
- updates
- clarification
- additional observations
- partial resolution
- follow-up reasoning

without creating unnecessary duplicate cases.

This is how cases remain living briefly without making the whole Social page permanently heavy.


---

## 12. Case Maturity Rules

A case should be created when an emerging thread becomes sufficiently mature.

Maturity may come from any combination of:
1. meaningful user discussion
2. coherent topic formation
3. similarity to prior emerging threads or prior cases
4. strong relevance to MD files / system guidance

This means a case does not require a large number of posts if the problem pattern is clearly case-worthy.

A case may be created from a relatively small amount of discussion if:
- the topic is coherent
- the issue is clearly relevant
- system guidance supports its importance

Cases come from **mature emerging threads**, not from the whole social session blob.


---

## 13. The Unit of Case Creation

The unit of case creation is the **Emerging Thread**, not the whole chat stream.

A single social session may contain:
- one feeding thread
- one ammonia/biofilter thread
- one skin-finding thread

These may become:
- one feeding case
- one ammonia/biofilter case
- one skin-finding case

Do not collapse distinct mature threads into one generic case.


---

## 14. Signal Types

Each post contribution should ideally be interpreted as one or more of:

- Observation
- Context
- Measurement
- Uncertain Measurement
- Suggestion / Hypothesis
- Recommendation
- Prior Experience
- Case Recall
- LLM-assisted Interpretation

These are not equal.

Example:
- “pH 6.8 on an uncalibrated meter” = **Uncertain Measurement**
- “I have heard frogs eat better if somewhat crowded” = **Suggestion / Hypothesis**
- “They are not approaching food as usual” = **Observation**

The system must preserve these distinctions.


---

## 15. Recommendation Support Categories

All recommendations or guidance points must be distinguishable by support type.

### 1. System-Supported
Grounded in:
- MD files
- stable system guidance
- foundational husbandry doctrine

### 2. Case-Supported
Grounded in:
- prior cases
- repeated case patterns
- stored case memory
- resolved or reinforced historical cases

### 3. Emerging Suggestion
Grounded in:
- live discussion
- current inference
- current weakly supported idea
- not yet strongly reinforced

The system must not present all recommendations as equally authoritative.


---

## 16. Negative Weighting / Poor-Fit Logic

Frog Social must not only detect what fits.

It must also de-emphasize explanations that do not fit well.

If a hypothesis:
- does not fit known husbandry patterns
- lacks expected supporting signs
- is weakly supported by current evidence
- conflicts with stronger interpretations

then it should remain:
- lower confidence
- de-emphasized
- or listed as an open question

It should not dominate case framing too early.

Example:
- reduced feeding alone should not strongly imply ammonia stress
- if ammonia stress were central, broader support might usually be expected
- if that support is missing, ammonia remains a weaker or open hypothesis


---

## 17. MD Files vs Cases

### MD Files
Use MD files for:
- foundational guidance
- stable husbandry truth
- system-level interpretive doctrine
- high-authority long-term guidance

Update MD files:
- carefully
- periodically
- intentionally
- by trusted maintainers

### Cases
Use cases for:
- applied knowledge
- situational truth
- rapid operational memory
- examples of what happened
- what was tried
- what seemed to help

A strong case can shape future recommendations.
But it is not automatically equal to MD doctrine.

This distinction must remain visible in the system.


---

## 18. Multi-User Requirement

Frog Social is a multi-user structured intelligence environment.

The system should care not only about **what** was said, but also:
- **who** said it
- whether another user reinforced it
- whether a reply is observation vs suggestion
- whether a topic is shared across multiple participants

This is important because:
- a repeated issue across multiple users is more meaningful than one person talking to themselves
- collaborative reinforcement is part of case-worthiness
- social structure is part of the intelligence pattern

Single-user testing is limited.
Real evaluation requires multiple logged-in users.


---

## 19. Social Page UI Rules

The Social page should remain readable and light.

It should show:
- persistent posts
- recent emerging threads
- up to 2 current cases
- optionally temporary related/recalled cases

It should not become:
- the archive
- cluttered with old threads
- cluttered with old case surfaces
- a giant memory wall

Case History is the archive.
The Social page is the live workspace.


---

## 20. Language Rules

The system should avoid:
- duplicated labels
- transcript-like titles
- clipped fragments
- spelling errors
- repeated phrases
- overclaiming
- AI-sounding certainty when support is weak

Preferred tone:
- structured
- careful
- field-note-like
- concise
- low-drama
- high-trust

Examples:
- prefer “suggests” over “confirms”
- preserve uncertainty when measurements are uncertain
- keep case titles normalized, not copied from raw posts

4. Describe a Problem also creates cases.

6. Current cases visible on Social are limited.

## 24. Privacy, Anonymity, and Content Protection

Frog Social is a privacy-conscious, login-gated platform.

### Content Protection
- User content may not be reposted, redistributed, or publicly attributed outside Frog Social without explicit permission from the poster.
- Platform use of content for internal AI/case logic is permitted; external redistribution is not.

### Anonymity Controls
- Users may post anonymously to the Social feed.
- Users may submit cases anonymously via Describe a Problem.
- Colony registrations default to private (visible only to owner/authorized group).
- Users may register with coded identities rather than real names.

### Login-Gating
- All contributions (posts, cases, colony records) require authentication.
- No content is indexed by public search engines.

Full terms and privacy rules are defined in: `docs/TERMS_AND_PRIVACY.md`

## 25. Colony Register + Management

Colony Management is a **strictly private**, lightweight system for users to maintain continuity records.

**Privacy boundary**: Each colony record belongs to one user (or their authorized lab group). No other Frog Social user can see, search, or access another user's colony data. This is enforced at the data layer — per-user storage with no cross-user queries.

It provides:
- colony/system registration with coded or real identifiers
- baseline system context (water source, system type, density, temp)
- running event log (imports, changes, treatments, observations)
- status/recovery tracking
- case linkage (colony history connected to Frog Social cases)
- exportable local record (CSV)

Colony Management is tightly connected to:
- **continuity** — running record of changes
- **troubleshooting** — immediate context when problems arise
- **case memory** — correlation between events and cases
- **system context** — AI uses baseline for better recommendations

Colony Management is NOT generic vivarium software. It exists to serve troubleshooting and case memory.

Full spec: `docs/social/COLONY_REGISTER_SPEC.md`

---

## 26. Describe a Problem — Progressive Disclosure

Describe a Problem uses a minimum-information-first model:

- Step 1 requires only: what's happening + which colony + anonymous choice
- All other fields are optional enrichment (collapsed by default)
- Users can return and add more detail over time
- The AI summary updates as more information is added
- No user is overwhelmed on first entry

Full spec: `docs/social/DESCRIBE_A_PROBLEM_SPEC.md`
